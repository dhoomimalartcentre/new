About
Dhoomimal

Dhoomimal began in 1936, when Ram Babu, a Sunday painter whose family ran a stationery business in Connaught Place, opened the Dhoomimal Dharam Das Gallery. Known to the city as the Kagazis, the family turned a paper shop into a meeting place for artists, supported by B C Sanyal, Jaya Appaswamy, Jamini Roy, Sailoz Mukherjee and Nand Lal Bose.

In the sixties Ram Babu's son Mahender Jain took the gallery abroad, showing Indian contemporary art in New York and across the United States. With his wife Sushma he worked closely with Shanti Dave, M F Husain, Tyeb Mehta and B Prabha, and helped set up Cymroza Art Gallery in Mumbai in 1974, and Chitrakoot Gallery in Calcutta and the House of B. Prabha & B. Vithal in New Delhi in 1987.

Three generations in Connaught Place

Mahender Jain died in 1987. Sushma Jain carried the gallery on with the same vision and devotion, keeping its doors open to the older masters and to younger artists finding their way.

Their son Mohit Jain joined in 1991 and took over in 1996, with his mother beside him. The centre's archive of photographs, from Indira Gandhi's visits to B C Sanyal's tribute in 1997, records how closely the gallery has grown with Delhi's art world.

Under Mohit Jain the gallery was renovated in 2007 and has shown at the India Art Fair and Art Mumbai. His curated exhibitions include The Millennium Mask, Half a Foot Square, Kites: A Celebration of Freedom and Portrait of an Artist.

Today the centre shows modern and contemporary Indian art in Connaught Place and beyond, publishes catalogues of its exhibitions, and offers art restoration services.
